What KnightShiftAI does

Read this part first

You upload a bid set. KnightShiftAI reads the drawings and schedules the way an estimator would — room by room, sheet by sheet — measures the paintable surfaces, prices them against your rates, and emails you back a full takeoff and estimate. You review the numbers, adjust anything you disagree with, and send the estimate on to your customer. It replaces the measuring, not your judgment.

Upload this

  • The full drawing set — floor plans, RCPs, elevations.
  • Finish schedules, room finish plans, door and window schedules. These drive the accuracy more than anything else.
  • One combined PDF of the whole set, if your set is split into volumes. It extracts better than separate files.

Don't bother with

  • A cover sheet alone. No plans, no takeoff.
  • Two projects in one submission. One project per upload — always.
  • Scans of scans. Original PDFs from the architect read far better than photographed or re‑scanned prints.
B

Your first bid, step by step

Start to finish

  1. 1
    Set your rates before you upload anything Open Pricing Defaults and put in your real numbers — walls, ceilings, doors, base trim, and your global markup. Every estimate is priced off these, so five minutes here is the difference between a usable first bid and a throwaway one.
  2. 2
    Submit the job on New Estimate Drag the PDFs in, name the job, set the due date, pick New Construction or Repaint. On the free plan: up to 5 files, 100 MB each.
  3. 3
    Write the scope notes like you'd brief a foreman The Additional Notes box is where you say what's in and what's out — "second floor only", "skip the basement", "no exterior". It's the single highest-leverage field on the form.
  4. 4
    Walk away The job moves to Active & Queued and runs on its own; a big set takes a while. We email you the moment it's done — you don't need to sit on the page.
  5. 5
    Review the line items, then send it Open the job under Completed. Check the annotated PDF to see what was counted and where. Adjust any rate or markup inline and the subtotal updates. When it's right, Send Estimate for Approval mails it to your customer.
C

The six tabs

Left to right, as they appear

✏️New Estimate
Where every job starts. Job name, due date, PDF upload, project type, and scope notes. A Rates & Markups panel sits alongside the form — it opens pre-filled with your account defaults, and anything you change there applies to this job only. On your phone, /mobile gives you the same upload form stripped down.
📊Active & Queued
Everything currently waiting or running, with live status. Nothing to do here but check progress — the estimate is emailed to you either way.
📁Completed
Your finished estimates, and where you'll spend your review time. Each job gives you the estimate PDF, the annotated PDF showing what was measured on which sheet, an editable line-item table with rate and markup fields, Send Estimate for Approval, and Re-run with revisions if a schedule was missing or a drawing came back updated. Every re-run is kept in the revision history.
📈Usage & ROI
Jobs run, total and average job value, average runtime, and estimator-time saved. Put your own estimator wage and typical hours-per-bid into the savings inputs and the payback math uses your numbers, not ours.
💰Pricing Defaults
Your account-wide rates: interior walls, ceilings, doors, windows, base trim, stairs, CMU, dryfall, concrete sealer, painted columns — plus a global markup entered as a decimal (0.06 = 6%). Specialty items (epoxy, precast, HM frames, handrails, faux finishes) can be switched on for your org when you need them. Set this up before your first upload.
✉️Message Settings
The default subject line, body, and standing CC/BCC list used when you send an estimate for approval. Write it once in your own voice and every estimate goes out sounding like your company.

Your name in the top-right corner opens the rest: Manage organization, Members (invite your estimators — they sign in with the invited email and join automatically), and sign out. The badge next to it counts down your remaining free bids.

Getting it right the first time

Where new users lose accuracy

Do

  • Set your rates first — a takeoff priced at someone else's numbers looks wrong even when the measurements are right.
  • Include every schedule you have. Missing schedules are the number-one cause of a soft number.
  • Spell out exclusions in the notes.
  • Open the annotated PDF before you judge the total. It shows you exactly what was counted and where.

Watch for

  • Anything flagged for review — that's the system telling you a number needs your eyes, and it's worth listening to.
  • Scope you know is on the plans but isn't in the line items. Use Re-run with revisions and point at the schedule that was missed.
  • Rates left at defaults on the job form. Per-job edits don't change your account defaults.
